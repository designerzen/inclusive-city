### Concept

This is a story about how a city designed for humans is problematic for robots.

Our research into making the world more accessible, equitable and inclusive is very important, but one of the main challenges we have is clearly communicating the complicated concepts. We think we have found a way to make our research into accessibility, itself accessible, by designing a game starring real robots that face barriers due to their differing capabilities—apparent limitations do not necessarily lie within the robot itself, they emerge from its relationship with the environment. 

There are three self-contained activities/chapters to our story.

Beginning
It begins by choosing a cardboard body for your robot from a choice of five very different looking ones. Each has its own capabilities and limitations, one may have vision, another hearing. There are five robot abilities in total and each robot inherits only three. With the provided art and craft supplies you are encouraged to customise and personalise them to make them totally unique; add colours, accessories, features, change shapes, add materials. We encourage people to get really experimental with their robot designs and provide diverse and flamboyant examples along with easy to follow instructions and guides. These personalisations, give your robot an entirely unique and bespoke personality, voice, fashion sense and style, which all then influence the way that they approach the world.

Whilst befriending your robot they tell you that they are an artist and a musician but as a robot they cannot make art or music without human help. First they must find their way through the city to the Studio, but this is not a city designed for robots.

Middle
The discussion turns to what issues and obstacles the robot might encounter in their journey – what kind of barriers might they face? People are asked to make predictions and suggestions.

Inside the human city your robot begins to make its way but encounters issues; a pavement without a ramp, a narrow doorway, a button just out of reach… what works for one robot does not work for another!

Luckily, this is a magic city where participants instantly become architects and town planners: every dimension and feature is under your control; pavements can be widened, ramps added, buttons can be lowered, audible signals set up, elevators installed… with careful consideration you can make the robot thrive in a city it was not designed for. Visitors observe their robots, redesign the environment then retest, becoming co-researchers in their own little accessibility experiment and your robot friend has some good suggestions to help you too.

You will have to adapt the city to enable your new friend to make their way through the human city to get to the studio.

Every robot has a different combination of capabilities and ways of experiencing the world. The same environment can work very differently for different robots and when the environment changes, it alters who can participate and what becomes possible.

Disabled researchers will help visitors connect these discoveries to real examples from inclusive technology research.

Once the city has been adapted enough for your robot they will head to the studio. Along the way, the robot will pick up supplies and inspiration for its artwork with artistic ideas inspired by the challenges, barriers and experiences they faced in their journey.

Finale
Our robots are acutely aware that they cannot make compelling music or art without the help of humans and so request your help in the final chapter—an artistic collaboration with your robot. During their adventures they make sounds that form together into melodies and rhythms based on their recent experiences. These seed a collaborative composition controlled by a broad variety of input mechanisms, this will be a truly multi-modal jam, using physical buttons, creative interfaces, motion capture and mood control.

This final activity gives the journey a memorable creative conclusion and demonstrates that difference is not simply something to accommodate.; it can also be a source of creativity and innovation, difference can generate new possibilities, not merely difficulties.

All of the created art and music will be available to take home, as well as your awesome robot design. We would love to add whimsical features such as a live-streamed catwalk for our robots, or a radio station for the music. There are many fun flourishes we would love to experiment with.


Conclusion : Key Messaging
There isn’t one right way to move, sense, communicate or create.
When we change the environment, we change who can participate and what
becomes possible.

Different ways of moving, sensing and communicating do not simply create different challenges, they can produce different creative possibilities and outcomes, demonstrating the principle at the heart of the project: different ways of experiencing the world produce valuable knowledge.

Different systems perceive and interact with the same environment differently.

Ability is not simply a characteristic of an individual or machine; environment and
design matter.

Changing one feature of an environment can dramatically alter an outcome.

Designing for difference can generate new possibilities rather than simply solving
“problems”.

Different ways of moving, sensing and communicating can also become different
ways of creating.

Summary
1. Build and personalise a robot unique to you - each robot has specific capabilities.
2. Improve a city by removing the barriers that affect its residents abilities through experimentation.
3. Make music and art with your robot, based around the robots experiences, using accessible multi-modal approaches.
