We have started exploring an idea that brings together Nic’s robots, Zen’s music and Kate’s art methods. Inspired by the Thomas and Friends Sodor Island delivery set, we imagined an interactive activity trail:
Design your robot: Our robots are disabled. Visitors give their robot a name, style and artistic identity, and choose three features relating to movement, sensing or communication. These could represent different ways of moving, perceiving and interacting with the world.

Navigate the environment: Visitors guide their robot through a route with obstacles, such as a bicycle blocking a pavement or roadworks closing a street. They explore how the robot’s features and the design of the environment affect its journey, and what changes would make the route more accessible.
Create music: The robot’s movements and route generate a musical pattern. At the music station, visitors add another layer using their own head or facial movements, creating a collaborative track.

The central question could be: How does the design of an environment affect who can move, participate and create?
We want visitors to leave thinking about accessibility, inclusion and different ways of contributing. The experience should help them recognise people’s strengths and creativity while understanding how barriers arise, and how design can remove them.

This is an initial concept for us to develop together. We need to clarify the research underpinning it, what visitors will discover, how the activities connect technically, and how we make the whole experience accessible
