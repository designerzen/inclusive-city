Draft Ideas
Different Robots, Different Routes
RIX Inclusive Research Royal Society Summer Science Exhibition 2027

Research Questions
What happens when different robots experience the same world?
Can changing the environment change what a robot is able to do?
Our interactive exhibit will explore how movement, sensing, communication and
creativity emerge from the relationship between an individual and their environment.
Rather than presenting some robots as “disabled”, every robot will have a different
combination of capabilities and ways of experiencing the world. Visitors will discover
that the same environment can work very differently for different robots and that
changing the environment can change who is able to participate.
Science
The exhibit brings together robotics, inclusive design, sensory research, human-
computer interaction, art and music.
Visitors become co- researchers. They can make a prediction, test their robot in an
environment, observe what happens, make changes and test again.
The experiment asks:
Is successful navigation determined by the capabilities of the robot, the design of the
environment, or the interaction between the two?
Our research connects how people experience environments differently and how
inclusive, sensory and participatory approaches can reveal barriers and possibilities
that conventional design may overlook.
Importantly, people with learning differences and disabilities are not the “subjects” of
the exhibit. Rix coresearchers purpleSTARS are co-researchers and co-designers
whose lived experience, sensory research and creative methods help shape the
questions, experiment and visitor experience.
What happens during a visit.
1. CREATE How does your robot experience the world?
Visitors meet/design a robot and give it a name and visual identity.

They choose a combination of capabilities relating to:

MOVMENT, SENSES, COMMUNICATION, CREATION
For example, robots might respond differently to sound, light, touch or obstacles;
move in different ways; or communicate through sound, movement or light.
There is deliberately no “normal” robot against which the others are measured.
2. PREDICT How will your robot manage this environment?
Visitors examine the route and predict what will happen.
Will their robot get through? Where might it encounter a barrier? What might help it?
3. TEST Send your robot into the world
Visitors guide or observe their robot navigating an interactive environment.
It could encounter features such as a narrow route, different surfaces, roadworks, a
blocked pavement or different sensory information.
Different robots will experience the same environment differently.
4. REDESIGN Change the world, not the robot
Instead of “fixing” their robot, visitors change the environment.
They might widen a route, move an obstruction, add a ramp, introduce a sensory
signal or create an alternative way through.
They then test the same robot again.
What changed? Why?
This creates a simple scientific cycle:
PREDICT → TEST → OBSERVE → REDESIGN → TEST AGAIN
5. CREATE Turn difference into music
The robot&#39;s movements, sensory encounters or routes generate musical information.
At Zen&#39;s music station, visitors can add another layer using accessible interfaces,
potentially including head, facial or body movements.
Different robots and different people therefore make different contributions to a
shared music/sound composition.
The activity ends not with identifying which robot performed “best”, but with
discovering what becomes possible when different forms of participation are
combined.

What will visitors discover?

Visitors should leave understanding that:
Different systems perceive and interact with the same environment differently.
Ability is not simply a characteristic of an individual or machine; environment and
design matter.
Changing one feature of an environment can dramatically alter an outcome.
Designing for difference can generate new possibilities rather than simply solving
“problems”.
Different ways of moving, sensing and communicating can also become different
ways of creating.
Rix coresearchers purpleSTARS research and exhibition design through established
approaches including:
sensory exploration • sensory mapping • accessible co-design • creative making •
sound • sensory objects • video • repeated observation • noticing environments
differently
Rix co-researchers purpleSTARS help identify and design the environmental
challenges, test the robots and visitor activities, develop accessible ways of
explaining the science, and potentially appear within or deliver the exhibit.
Their involvement demonstrates the principle at the heart of the project: different
ways of experiencing the world produce valuable knowledge.
The take-home message
There isn&#39;t one right way to move, sense, communicate or create.
When we change the environment, we change who can participate and what
becomes possible.
