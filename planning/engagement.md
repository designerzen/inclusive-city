### Public Engagement

Rather than watching a demonstration, visitors become active participants in a story!
We have a compelling game with a strong story that uses clever analogies and
illustrative parallels to communicate complicated concepts, both social and scientific, in
a simple and straightforward, enjoyably experiential, and memorable manner.
Play is a gateway for learning, so our exhibit revolves around a fun multi-modal game
presented as a city, with a captivating and relatable narrative. Participants befriend a
robot in a cardboard outfit that they personalise not just with googly eyes and glitter,
but with structure, shape and abilities that affect how that robot then moves and
experiences its world.
Customisation
We will use crafts as it has no right or wrong, no competitive rivalry, and engages the
creative brain. We use this friendly safe space to get everybody comfortable and to ask
if they have any access requirements, needs or concerns. Celebrating diversity, we
encourage as many different looking and different shaped robots as possible and we
will take photos for our robot gallery.
Personalisation is key to participants establishing a bond with their new robot friend but
they also provide a base seed for each robot’s custom voice and sound effects, giving
the robot unique character and charm and making their journey more appealing and
engaging.
Inclusive participation
When the robot encounters a barrier, the participant can change the environment or
interaction method and immediately observe the consequences. This hands-on
experimentation will make the relationship between capabilities and environmental
design tangible. The control system will be as malleable as possible with a range of
input strategies.
Learning through discovery
Experiment! Hypothesize and make predictions, test, discover access issues from the
environment, change them and retry. This progression must be self-evident and
apparent to the participant, but we will also be there with spoken commentary and
suggestions.
Creativity and collaboration
Each journey will contribute to a musical pattern, which visitors can extend personally
through accessible controls and complement through novel multi-modal musical input
methods, allowing them to collaborate with the robot and create meaningful
takeaways.

An experience that stays with visitors
Creating a shared piece of music will offer an enjoyable way to discover that people
can contribute through different forms of interaction. We reward participation and
visitors get to take their robot designs home, as well as the artwork and music they
subsequently made with them... plus there is a video game version of the game
featuring their robot! We want this experience to be memorable, inspiring and long
lasting.
Everything will be very inviting and designed with simplicity and communication in
mind, with big flashing buttons, clear instructions, fun sounds, compelling characters
and a powerful narrative to drive visitors through the whole experience.
Rix research topics includes accessible events and inclusion, and we hope to use this
opportunity to help expand our own research by trialing certain new tech that will
feature in our future research and by listening to the public feedback.
Most importantly, we hope visitors will leave with a different mindset, rather than,
"What is someone unable to do?", they may begin asking, "What could we change to
make participation possible?"
