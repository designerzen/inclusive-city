# Procedural journey painting

Composition now uses independent named seed streams, following the per-purpose seed approach in [Highlight's generative-art utilities](https://github.com/highlightxyz/generative-art/blob/0bc3b551e5a48abf04a56c03276b0fc7a02da4f8/hl-utils.js) and [advanced p5 example](https://github.com/highlightxyz/generative-art/blob/0bc3b551e5a48abf04a56c03276b0fc7a02da4f8/examples/advanced-p5-dice-example/sketch.js). `paintField.ts` is an independent Canvas/worker-safe implementation; it does not embed Highlight code or require its token runtime. Each run chooses one of six spatial grammars in `paintingComposition.ts`: layered horizons, orbital arcs, branching botanical forms, patchwork fields, vertical calligraphy, or scattered constellations. Each grammar resolves its own cubic paths, scale contrasts and sparse structural motifs (ridges, discs, leaves or paper planes). Ten default pigment palettes and five paper grounds broaden the range. Family, motifs and the first mark's ground are stored in the JSON score; older scores without these optional fields still render. The selected painter brush language operates over the chosen grammar, giving variety within the same painter style. Event tension and relief still shape each gesture. Smooth coordinate noise guides the brush and gives ribbons and watercolour tide edges correlated irregularity. This noise extension is our own technique, rather than a feature of Highlight's dice artwork. Normalized geometry keeps studies, HUD images and exports consistent; saved resolved marks remain replayable without rerunning composition.

Journey music now develops through Markov chains, cellular automata, stochastic distributions and evolutionary motif selection. See the [development algorithms, musical behaviour and runtime bounds](MusicDevelopment.md).

`ProceduralPainting` consumes the current run's complete ordered robot event stream. Painting begins immediately, independent of music or art pickups. Every `step` creates one new curved pigment stroke. Barriers add fractured charcoal; interventions create golden blooms; pickups add blossoms; achievements leave luminous seals; route segments, queued detours, pause/resume and interrupted journeys add ink gestures. Arrival adds a finishing flourish. Idle time produces no new marks.

The robot's identity/configuration seeds its palette and composition. Position, heading, edge, step count and event order shape the painting, rather than copying the route onto the canvas. Drawing spark enriches bristle texture, opacity and stroke breadth; Colour prism expands the subsequent pigment palette. Earlier paint stays intact.

The designer's **Creative personality** selects one of eighteen painter styles and fifty-six musician styles. Painter choices are Impressionist, Watercolourist, Expressionist, Cubist, Geometric abstract artist, Pointillist, Ink painter, Surrealist, Pop artist, Minimalist, Collage artist, Dada, Bauhaus, Art Nouveau, Art Deco, Suprematism, Op Art and Abstract Expressionism. Music choices are Melodic explorer, Ambient, Classical piano, Minimalist, Jazz, Blues, Synth-pop, Techno, Bells/chimes, Chiptune, Folk, Waltz, Bossa nova, Cinematic, Lo-fi, Baroque counterpoint, Romantic piano, Ragtime, Funk, Reggae/dub, Disco/house, Synthwave and Drum & bass. These are original generative interpretations using Canvas brushes and oscillator instruments, with a visual study and four-bar musical audition in the designer. Existing `electronic` and `latin` IDs remain compatible with saved Synth-pop and Bossa nova settings.

`artistStyles.ts` defines the catalogue. `worldMusicStyles.ts` adds 33 arrangements spanning African, Middle Eastern, South and East Asian, European, Latin American, Caribbean and American styles: Afrobeat, Highlife, Soukous, Mbalax, Ethio-jazz, Amapiano, Gnawa-inspired, Raï-inspired, Arabic maqam-inspired, Turkish folk-inspired, Persian classical-inspired, Indian raga-inspired, Qawwali-inspired, Gamelan-inspired, Japanese koto-inspired, Chinese guzheng-inspired, Korean folk-inspired, Irish jig-inspired, Flamenco-inspired, Klezmer-inspired, Balkan dance-inspired, Tango, Argentine tango, Samba, Salsa, Cumbia, Merengue, Calypso, Soca, Bluegrass, Rock, Hip-hop and Trap. Each supplies its own tempo, lead voice, modal palette, melodic figure, bass, chord placement and percussion. These are original synthesized sketches; equal-tempered modal palettes do not reproduce microtonal tuning, complete ragas or traditional instruments. Turkish folk and Balkan dance use seven beats; Korean folk and Irish jig use three beats, with the jig dividing each beat into two pulses. `artistBrushes.ts` supplies distinct seeded drawing rules; every mark records its style so worker rendering, finished snapshots and PNG exports preserve the same appearance. `JourneyMusicComposer` controls phrasing, tempo, note selection, swing, chord extensions and accompaniment. Waltz uses three beats; blues follows a twelve-bar progression; jazz uses ii–V–I colours and swung eighths. Classical uses Alberti bass; Baroque adds a contrary melodic voice; Romantic uses rippling broken chords; Ragtime alternates bass and offbeat chord strikes. Funk uses sixteenth-note syncopation; Reggae uses offbeat skanks and a third-beat drum accent; Disco has four-on-the-floor kicks, offbeat hats and octave bass; Drum & bass uses fast broken beats. Synth-pop and Synthwave use repeating hooks and electronic backbeats. Percussion is oscillator-synthesized and saved as ordinary note scores.

Robot identity seeds a four-bar melodic contour and articulation pattern, plus its own waveform, envelope, filter, resonance, unison spread and vibrato. Genre rhythms and accompaniments remain recognisable; successive sections answer the original motif. Steps preserve the hook; phrase endings reflect route progress, and barriers introduce minor harmony. Harmony pickups enrich chord extensions. `JourneyCreativity.previewMusic()` demonstrates the selected genre with a stable robot audition seed. Live journeys receive a fresh saved composition seed, including on restart; replay keeps the exact resolved music. Robot voice variation preserves the genre waveform instead of replacing it. World styles retain their modal melody palettes during ordinary city moods. Resolved scores, chosen styles and the run's configuration snapshot remain separate from subsequent robot edits; score replay does not rerun the composer. Cached robots retain their individual selections, and ability Reset leaves artistic preferences intact.

`ArtworkTitleGenerator` remixes recognizable fragments from two different famous artworks into a poetic title. Robot seed, step count, discoveries, improvements and the ordered stroke seeds determine the pairing. The versioned title and its two inspirations are saved in `RobotRun.creative.title` at completion and reused on the catwalk and exhibition; replay never renames an existing work.

Brushes develop the run's spatial grammar, reposition between gestures, and alternate tentative touches with broad overlapping gestures. Barriers leave lingering tension in subsequent dark, restless strokes; discoveries and access improvements restore warmer, more open gestures. Each stroke records pressure, wetness, energy, tilt and lift alongside its geometry. Tapered pigment ribbons, ragged stains, broken dry bristles, pooling and splashes replace uniform-width lines and repeated radial stamps. All variation is seeded and recorded, so expressive output remains reproducible.

Each `PaintStroke` is a versioned, JSON-safe instruction containing normalized cubic control points, brush width, pigment/accent colours, opacity, texture seed and source event information. `RobotRun.creative.marks` keeps these resolved instructions. To restore a finished painting:

```ts
const painting = new ProceduralPainting(saved.seed);
painting.marks.push(...structuredClone(saved.marks));
PaintingRenderer.render(context, canvas.width, canvas.height, painting);
```

`PaintingRenderer` renders textured paper, translucent pigment washes, dry bristles, ink fractures and decorative blooms entirely with Canvas 2D. Its live renderer caches completed strokes and draws the current wet stroke progressively over roughly 250 ms. The saved painting is independent of frame rate. Reduced motion commits marks immediately. Catwalk rendering uses the same strokes at a higher resolution, without an animated brush overlay. Cross-browser bit-identical pixels are not promised.

`AsyncPaintingRenderer` runs live strokes, finished snapshots and PNG encoding in a dedicated module worker using `OffscreenCanvas`. Only new marks are sent for live frames, with one frame request in flight at a time; settled paintings stop rendering until the score or canvas size changes. Completed `ImageBitmap`s are copied onto the HUD and closed immediately. Restarting, leaving and disposing terminate the worker and reject outstanding requests. Unsupported or failed workers fall back to the same deterministic renderer on the main thread.

The exhibition recreates its artist from the completed run's appearance and profile, standing in front of the painting. `JourneyDance` derives choreography from saved note onsets, pitches, gain, tempo and meter, sampled against `CitySounds.perform()`'s audible output clock. Motion is independent of frame accumulation; quiet gaps settle, playback stop/finish returns to a presentation pose, and reduced motion keeps the robot still. The separate transparent 3D canvas does not enter the painting PNG. Its scene is disposed on exit and only renders while dancing or when a still frame needs refreshing.

The HUD stays visible throughout the journey, with a stroke count and a short explanation of the latest mark. The canvas has a text description; mobile layouts place it beneath the city view. Hovering or focusing either artwork reveals a PNG download button (always visible on touch screens), exporting a frozen 2400 × 1200 snapshot of the score. No images, fonts or random external assets are downloaded.

Journey events colour the harmony with determination (major sixth), uncertainty (suspended seventh), frustration (half-diminished), sadness (minor seventh), relief (major add-nine), wonder (Lydian major seventh), happiness (sixth/ninth), and celebration (major seventh/ninth/sixth). Pause settles into a major seventh. Event colours last two bars; blocked, waiting, paused and arrived states sustain their colour. Reactions follow the scheduled bar’s harmony, with simultaneous chord accents for significant encounters; arrival resolves into a celebration chord. The studio keyboard, saved performance and MP3 export preserve each original synth patch instead of replacing the arrangement with one piano sound.

Argentine tango is a separate orchestral interpretation alongside the original Tango rhythmic sketch. It adds bandoneón-like reed phrasing, a marked four-beat bass, piano accents and sustained bowed-synth answers, without a drum-machine pulse. Both are original oscillator arrangements, not recordings or faithful acoustic instrument emulations. Background: [Todo Tango listening guide](https://www.todotango.com/english/history/chronicle/448/listening-to-tango-dance-music-a-beginners-guide/) and [Argentine instrumental tango research](https://cilam.ucr.edu/diagonal/issues/2013/LinkandWendland.pdf).
